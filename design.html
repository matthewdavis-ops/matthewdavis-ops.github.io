Clinical, Regulatory & Brand

Design

None of this is a gallery. Every piece was made to do a specific job — make a federal coverage rule usable at the bedside, let a partner hospital put its own name on something, teach a safety standard to someone who has never opened a code book. The system matters as much as the artifact: one illustration library feeds the layouts, the layouts carry blank slots, and the whole thing scales to a network of facilities without a designer in the loop.

Point-of-Decision Tools

Each one takes a rule — a federal coverage criterion, a pressure-vessel standard, a proposed payment model — and makes it actionable at the moment someone has to act on it.

Partner-Enablement Systems

Built to be used under somebody else’s name. The blank spaces are the point — a partner facility earns the reach under its own banner rather than the parent company’s.

WOUNDWISE — twenty-three units. Each one pairs original branded artwork with a complete content kit: suggested article topics, ready-to-post captions, conversation starters, platform-specific hashtag sets, and a quick-start guide written for clinic staff with no marketing background. Deliberately de-branded, for the same reason as the flyer above.

The Network

Architectural illustrations of partner facilities, drawn for proposals, planning documents and partner materials across a seven-state footprint.

Twelve facilities. Each one drawn from the real building — the thread that runs back to Draftsman, 2009, laying out wound care and hyperbaric centers for contracted hospitals.

Before All of It

The design work didn’t start at a hospital. It started with a technical module in sixth grade, around 1998, and never stopped — straight through the battery plant, the organ shop and the heat-treat lab.

A large part of it was built for the web, by hand, between 2011 and 2020 — a band’s recording studio drawn as a clickable room, a Myst-style personal world, a furniture business, and a novelist’s catalog kept current a book at a time. Five sites, screenshotted from the original files →
