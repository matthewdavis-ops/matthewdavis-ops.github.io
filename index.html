Summary

I specialize in problems that don’t yet have clear ownership, language, or structure. My work sits at the intersection of operations, strategy, compliance, technology, communications, and organizational design.

Over the past two decades, I’ve repeatedly been asked to step into situations where the root problem was not yet well understood, create clarity around what was actually happening, and leave behind systems, processes, documentation, and architecture that continue working after my direct involvement ends.

That work has ranged from metallurgical quality control on drivetrain components to multi-state healthcare operations — by way of regulatory compliance, business development, information security, training systems, data architecture, communications, and independent research. The specific domain changes. The underlying work remains the same.

Transform complexity into clarity. Convert that clarity into durable execution.

Turnaround & Corporate Development

  • Helped drive a post-pandemic turnaround — rebuilding to profitability within roughly two years after a $250K shortfall and the loss of half the company’s contracts, with a corporate staff of four.
  • Rebuilt the company’s business model around its own IP rather than its payroll — from placing staff in partner facilities and billing per procedure, to equipping partners’ own clinical teams with the company’s documentation, compliance and training systems under a flat annual fee. Built once and deployed per partner, the cost of each additional partnership fell to near nothing, which let a four-person company compete for contracts in a price band the national chains could not profitably serve.
  • Built the financial model the company underwrote its partnerships with — a reimbursement engine that prices a proposed wound care and hyperbaric program from the CMS payment rules up: published payment weights procedure by procedure, wage-adjusted to the individual hospital on the labor-related share, run against a demand forecast built zip code by zip code from census and published prevalence data, and a staffing model drawn from the company’s own multi-hospital census history. Hospital executives used the output to decide whether to open the service line.
  • Built the company’s market-intelligence and demand-generation function from nothing. A national census of every facility class the company could serve — hospital systems, independent hospitals, multispecialty physician practice groups, group purchasing organizations, and every critical access and long-term acute care hospital in the country — mapped against competitor presence site by site, down to each facility’s executive team by name and role. Fed by website analytics I resolved to the nearest facility and logged every weekday morning before the day started, scored by engagement depth and repeat visits, and distributed to a field sales team as a daily lead sheet. Ran it by hand until the process was proven, then brought on a data systems hire and had it automated.
  • Closed the bulk of the company’s consulting partnerships — hospital and physician-group accounts across five states — owning each from inbound lead to signed multi-year contract, and the deal work behind them.
  • Built the referral-development system partner facilities run on — a clinical education library indexed to covered indications and routed by physician specialty, so a vascular surgeon, a radiation oncologist and a podiatrist each receive the evidence for what they actually see, paired with the outreach method for working the territory. Deployed per facility under the partner hospital’s own brand, not the company’s.
  • Built the company’s response to a multi-period IRS Employee Retention Credit examination, assembling the financial and payroll documentation and addressing every information request for CPA and counsel to file.
