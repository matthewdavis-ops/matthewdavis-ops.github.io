The HBOT Screening System

The original concept was a single screening tool covering the CMS NCD 20.29 hyperbaric indications we saw most often in our centers. The problem was usability. Staff only needed one indication, which meant navigating six irrelevant sections to reach the one that applied to their patient, with little to no room to work.

So I split it into seven indication-specific tools, each carrying the coverage criteria for that condition. Rather than telling a reviewer whether a patient qualifies, each tool identifies which required elements are still missing from the medical record and translates those gaps into actionable next steps.

Two of the seven indications depend on documentation that may not exist. Radionecrosis patients often arrive through outside referral sources, and their radiation therapy may have occurred decades earlier. While requests for treatment records are sent, those records do not always follow. To account for that, referring providers complete a signs-and-symptoms checklist during the referral process, giving HBOT technicians something to screen against before the tool is ever opened.

The gaps carry forward to a watch and potential list the hyperbaric staff reviews with the wound care team — daily at some centers, weekly at others. Patients who do not yet qualify stay visible, with the reason documented, rather than disappearing from the process.

The tools also state what does not qualify for coverage. In my experience, knowing where the boundary is matters as much as knowing what falls inside it — the boundary is where programs get burned.
